# Design review: 3 October 2026

Repository: `benhitchins-stack/ev-site-planner` at `930956c` (after the guide library and help drawer, PR #29).

This review asks one question: what set of design changes would most improve the planner for UK installers on site and estimators at a desk, now that the navy and lime shell, the stage bar, the project cards, the help drawer, the guide library and site audits have shipped. It does not re-validate electrical calculations or regulatory content, and it does not propose accounts, sync or a backend.

## Verdict

The shell is in good shape. Home, Overview, the project pages, the Markup tool strip, the panels and the review dialogs now read as one product, lime is used with discipline, and the five-stage model is applied consistently with deep links. The work that remains is not another visual pass. It sits in three places the recent redesign did not reach:

1. **What reaches the client.** The PDFs carry almost none of the brand, print placeholders and "Not recorded", greet a company by its first word, describe the audit pack as an unreviewed draft, and date every revision with the day it was generated. This is where an installer wins or loses trust, and where a paying customer will judge the product.
2. **The seams between the new shell and the inherited planner.** The dialogs on the Technical menu do not close on Escape, keep focus behind them and stack on top of each other; the charging simulator chart escapes its dialog; two charging models give two different answers; a green "within supply" verdict rests on a hard-coded 200 A fallback; the cable calculator says "No cable runs drawn yet" on a plan with two trenches.
3. **The iPad.** At 1024 px, the primary field device, the sidebar loses its labels, the category strip clips Notes (where the snag pin lives), the scale status disappears, the inspector covers the item just selected, and the client pack's editing column is cut off at the dialog edge.

Most of the fixes are small. Of the 90 verified findings, 63 are under a day each. The recommended order below puts the cheap, visible, trust-affecting fixes first, then the tablet workspace, then one vocabulary for checking, then the design-system consolidation that stops the drift recurring.

## How this review was done

- **Evidence.** Every route, dialog, drawer, tab and review was captured with the example project at 1440 px desktop, 1024 px touch tablet and 390 px touch phone in Chromium (about 150 screenshots). All six PDF types and the PNG export were generated and rasterised. Per-page metrics recorded headings, unnamed controls, small targets, font sizes and overflow. No console errors or failed requests occurred on any page, and `npm test` passed (107 of 107).
- **Twelve lenses.** Independent reviewers looked through visual design and brand, information architecture, Markup interaction, tablet, phone, accessibility, content, documents, design system, onboarding, Design lab and data display, and guides and help. They returned 95 findings after merging duplicates.
- **Two skeptics per finding.** One checked that the problem is really present in the screenshots and code and not already fixed; the other checked that it is worth doing and that the recommendation fits the constraints. A finding survived only if both agreed. 90 survived, 2 were rejected and 3 did not complete both checks before a usage limit (listed at the end).
- **Founder context.** The 52 design transcripts in `ev-site-planner-history` were read for stated preferences. The recurring asks were a tool that is "not visually overwhelming" (chat 45), less hidden in menus (chat 21), no clipping on mobile (chats 10, 17, 52, including "Engineer pack - we have clipping weird text"), less palette scrolling (chat 34), and complex explanations behind ? buttons (chat 17). The recommendations below follow those.

Limits: Chromium automation only, no physical iPad, iPhone, Safari or assistive-technology run; one example project with one plan; the screenshot file names quoted in the appendix refer to that capture set, which is not committed.

## Decisions for the owner

These cannot be settled by design review alone.

1. **Primary button colour (F21).** The 2 October pass made panel footer primaries navy, but most of the product uses blue and the original planner did too. Navy on a navy-headed dialog looks secondary. Recommendation: blue for the primary action everywhere, lime only on navy, navy for selected states.
2. **One charging model (F07).** Recommendation: keep Design lab's Charging day and retire the Markup simulator's separate model, or make it read Design lab's assumptions.
3. **Site audit as a separate kind of project (F45).** Recommendation: give audits their own four-step strip and keep them out of the design stage bar.
4. **Client contact name (F15).** The fix needs a new optional field. Recommendation: add "Client contact name" to the project details.

## What not to do

- Do not rewrite the inherited planner or adopt a framework. Extract behind the existing browser tests, as the 2 October deep review recommends.
- Do not restyle the legacy dialogs again. Fix their behaviour (F03).
- Do not add a second "Next step" pill. Drive the existing primary button (F48).
- Do not auto-open the tour; `docs/guide-library.md` decided it stays opt-in.
- Do not raise the sidebar auto-collapse threshold at 1024 px; label the collapsed rail instead (F11).
- Do not put Plan settings tabs in a horizontal scroller; off-screen tabs are not found (F49).
- Do not remove any no-certification wording; remove only the release status (F17).
- Do not make the PDFs navy-heavy. Keep the white, installer-branded header and fix the cover hierarchy (F37).
- Do not add a new revision store; wire up `pack.revHistory` (F18).

## Strengths to protect

- The navy header, eyebrow and stage bar are applied identically across Overview, Design lab, Programme, Snags, Site audit and Issue, and each stage is a working link.
- Overview's Next actions open the exact fix: the unscaled plan with the scale tool, the file picker, the armed snag tool.
- Lime is used only as a fill on white and as text on navy, as `docs/page-redesign.md` intended.
- Status colour is restrained: lime for done, amber for attention, blue for active, coloured edges rather than coloured blocks.
- The plan, programme and client pack reviews share one frame with a preview that matches the download. It is the right template for the remaining legacy dialogs.
- Reference pills with leader lines, tap-to-place with a hint, stable pinch zoom and "Place another like this" are a sound direct-manipulation core.
- The home hero and returning welcome band are the strongest expression of the brand. The PDFs should borrow from them.

### 1. Fix what looks broken

#### F01. Global canvas rule leaks into dialogs: simulator chart escapes and uses an off-brand dark style
*High severity, small effort. Area: markup. Raised by: visual-brand, tablet, design-system, design-lab-dataviz.*

When correctly positioned the chart is still a near-black panel with 9px monospace labels inside a white Hanken Grotesk dialog. An installer showing a client the load curve on an iPad sees a broken screen.

**Recommendation.** 1. Fix now (one line, verified): add `#simCv{position:static;inset:auto;touch-action:auto}` next to the `#sldWrap canvas` override at EV Site Planner.html:1032 (height:260px is already inline). touch-action:auto matters because the dialog is overflow:auto and on phone it is taller than the viewport, so a finger-drag starting on the chart must scroll it. 2. Then, as the structural fix, scope the base rule at :147 to `#cvwrap canvas` (the only canvas under #stage; .fpe-cv and #c3dCanvas already have their own absolute rules, #peCanvas is sized by JS at :14108-14110) and trim only the `position:static;inset:auto` (and their !important) declarations from the eight undo rules (inline :1028 and :1032; workbench.css :98, :114, :122, :302; workspace.css :20; planning.css #evpPlanPreview), keeping their width/height/border/background/zoom declarations. 3. Test to match whichever option ships: with scoping, a static assertion in tests/site-static.test.mjs (assert.doesNotMatch on a bare /\ncanvas\{position:absolute/ in the planner HTML); with only the one-line fix, a browser assertion in presentation.browser.cjs that #simCv's bounding rect is inside .wlc after openSim(). 4. Restyle drawSimChart for parity with the SLD canvas and the Design lab's .evp-chart (light panel, 11-12px labels in var(--font) read via getComputedStyle, ink #435867, blue charging fill, slate standing load, amber dashed DLM, var(--ev-bad) supply cap); move the three in-chart text labels ("off-peak", "supply", "DLM limit") to a small chip row above the canvas so they stop colliding at 302px width; re-check on all three viewports since the chart sizes from clientWidth. Also note separately that the product now has two charging-day simulations (this dialog and the Design lab tab) with different visual languages; this restyle should not pre-empt a later decision on converging them.

**Evidence.** desktop-11, tablet-11, phone-11 markup-dialog-charging-simulator.png (chart pinned full-width at top of viewport over the top bar; dialog header pushed below; phone capture covers Peak demand and Verdict chips).
